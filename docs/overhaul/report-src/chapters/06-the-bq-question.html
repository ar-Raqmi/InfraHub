The BQ question, explained

One menu, no date on it

Why “what happens when a new contract comes?” has no good answer in InfraHub today, told first as a restaurant story and then as the real data flow.

The analogy

Pustaka = the menu. Project BQ = the receipt. Contract = the dated price list the menu should belong to.

A receipt keeps the price you paid, even if the menu changes tomorrow. That part of InfraHub works.

The trouble is that the restaurant has only one menu, with no date on it. If you order one more dish on an old bill, the waiter reads today’s menu. And when the owner reprints the menu, the old one goes in the bin: nobody can later prove what last year’s prices were.

The real flow

How a rate travels today, and the three places it is re-read

Follow a rate from the PT’s Pustaka screen into a project. Green arrows are copies, which are safe. The three amber squares are the moments the editor reaches back into the library and takes today’s rate, whatever contract the project belongs to.

1 · Tambah Item. There is no blank-item button: every row starts from the library, which is why 100% of rows carry a link. The PJA can still type over the rate.

2 · Apply a template. Rate and unit are overwritten with the library’s current values; if the link is dead, the template’s old stored rate is used silently.

3 · Pelarasan addition. New rows (isAdjustment) take the library rate of that day, not the project’s contract rate. 489 such rows exist today.
