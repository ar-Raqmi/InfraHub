UI/UX direction A · pipeline-first

Saluran

The six-status pipeline becomes the home screen. Every project is a card moving left to right, carrying its contract chip and its age in the stage.

The idea

A strip on top shows the median wait per stage, so bottlenecks such as Menunggu Lantikan and Tuntutan Bayaran show without a report. The project page repeats the six steps as a stepper and puts the contract binding right under the title. The four “phases” become “who acts” inside each status: one vocabulary.

Type and colour

Brand emerald and teal on a cool off-white. Stages are one sequential green ramp (deeper means further along). Amber is reserved for “kadar berubah” and slow stages; MPS purple for “kontrak baharu”.

What to look at

  1. The contract chip on every card (SH/302/25 · K2026).
  2. The rate-change queue in the BQ editor: old and new rate, %, RM effect, a choice per item.
  3. The contract lanes Draf / Aktif / Ditutup and the inline Klon kontrak form.

Pros

  • The pipeline becomes the navigation, so status is never hidden in a dropdown again.
  • A contract chip on every card shows a project on the wrong rate book across the whole list.
  • Closest to what the PT already does in his head: “which pile is this file in”.

Cons

  • With ~485 projects a year, columns need virtual scrolling and filters; Siap must collapse into a summary (as drawn).
  • A BQ is still a table: kanban adds little to the BQ editor itself.
  • On a phone a 6-column board must become a stage picker plus a list: a second layout to build.

Serves best

The PT admin and the Jurutera, who move projects between stages and chase the slow ones.

Effort · Yiyi’s estimate, not measured

About 5–7 developer-weeks of UI on top of the shared contract groundwork. Reuses most of the existing BQ editor internals.

Answers audit issues

5, 6 (one pipeline, visible ages) and 1, 2, 4 (contract chip, change queue, contract lanes).
