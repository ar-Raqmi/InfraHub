The one decision this report is about

How should InfraHub represent a contract?

Today a contract is only a text label (no_sebutharga) chosen in phase 2, after the BQ was already priced from one global price list. Nothing connects the label to any rate. It has not hurt yet because InfraHub has only ever lived under one contract: 420 of the 422 projects with a number carry MPS/SH/302/25.

The recommendation is to make the contract a real record that owns its own rate book (Option B, chapter 08), so a project’s rates come from the contract its work order was issued under, never from today’s date. Two cheap safety steps should come first (chapter 09). Your answers in chapter 90 decide the details.

In one paragraph

InfraHub is a tidy React app over a small API and one SQLite database. The workflow is a status label that a company field and three dates move forward. The money rules work, but live inside one 1,790-line form. The slow part of a project is the paperwork after the site, not the site. And the BQ library is a single undated price list: project BQs are safe copies of it, but every new item, template or pelarasan addition reads today’s list. The day the next sebutharga arrives with new rates, open projects will start mixing two contracts without anyone noticing.

Needs your answerOnly you (or the PT, Jurutera, Kewangan) can settle it. Collected as a checklist in chapter 90.

What the colours mean

Purple Anything that is a contract: sebutharga, rate book, Jadual Kadar Harga.
Emerald, teal InfraHub itself: code, tables, statuses, the system doing its job.
Amber A risk, a warning or a place where things can go wrong.
Slate Secondary information and still-open drafts.
